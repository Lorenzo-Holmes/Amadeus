# Candidate closure and conditional inference

Removing some proposed explanations does not establish that the remaining list covers every possible explanation. The reasoning representation now separates listed candidates, premise-bound exclusions, unresolved remainder, finite-universe closure basis and the scope of that basis. Domain coverage is explicit; evidence for one domain does not silently close another.

An explicitly finite hypothetical universe can support conditional list subtraction within its declared scope. Proposed premises, exclusions and closure descriptions remain unverified and cannot admit facts, completed actions or real-world exhaustiveness. Paused and reopened scopes do not continue narrowing automatically, and constructing a new description does not rewrite its predecessor.

Every actual context now includes a compact unresolved candidate-closure state, including inputs without lexical cues. Null fields mean unparsed information, so original conditions and quotations still have to be read. The full dialogue remains bound to its evidence index. Host JSON whitespace is compacted to preserve existing context-budget behavior; no input-budget or acceptance threshold is expanded.

The accompanying fixtures are newly authored, non-heldout examples spanning scientific diagnosis, software faults, planning and finite games. Offline tests check conditional closure, open remainders, domain separation, authority boundaries, actual transport projection and unchanged budget limits. Passing those checks does not establish target-model semantic acceptance; fresh complete evaluations remain required.

Provider terminal classification, network route and formal generation policy are unchanged by this repair. Historical requests and reviews remain immutable and cannot supply replacement replies or inherited passes.
